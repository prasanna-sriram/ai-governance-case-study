# Northglass Life — Fictional Company Profile

[Project overview](/README.md)

## Scenario premise

A fictional federally regulated Canadian life insurer. This profile defines the learning scenario. No incorporation, registration, size, revenue, customer count or regulatory finding is asserted about a real organisation.

## Business situation

The assistant initially retrieves approved internal claims guidance and drafts notes for trained staff. Its interface and proposed integrations could make recommendations increasingly influential. The case follows the difference between a narrow description such as “only drafts” and the authority supplied by the actual workflow.

## Question under review

What must change when an internal AI assistant starts influencing customer-impacting decisions?

## Systems and information

The chapters introduce only the systems, flows and permissions needed for the decision. A diagram, record or numerical example is a fictional representation; missing facts remain unknown until explicitly supplied. Avoid treating illustrative details as a complete architecture or risk inventory.

## Scope and boundaries

The assistant is not initially authorized to adjudicate claims or send customer decisions. Test examples are synthetic. An AI policy, framework crosswalk or aggregate metric does not establish conformity, fairness or safe deployment. Specialist model validation is not claimed by the author.

## Assessment approach

Start with the operation or intended use. Identify the failure path and potential impact, inspect proportionate evidence, evaluate response options, and state who can decide. Keep open issues and the limits of a test visible.

## Scenario maintenance

If a later case changes a purpose, dependency, permission or regulatory premise, state that change in the case and explain whether earlier conclusions still apply. Additions to the fictional setting are not changes to external law.
