# The Assistant with Too Much Authority: AI Governance

**A case-study project by Prasanna Sriram · AI governance**

What must change when an internal AI assistant starts influencing customer-impacting decisions?

The assistant initially retrieves approved internal claims guidance and drafts notes for trained staff. Its interface and proposed integrations could make recommendations increasingly influential. The case follows the difference between a narrow description such as “only drafts” and the authority supplied by the actual workflow.

## Purpose

Use-case boundaries, data and action permissions, lifecycle ownership, evaluation, human oversight, supplier changes, privacy implications and accountable deployment decisions. The project uses fictional situations to connect framework concepts with evidence, trade-offs and accountable decisions.

## Fictional setting

**Northglass Life** is a fictional federally regulated Canadian life insurer. [Read the company profile](/company_profile.md) for the operation, stakeholders, assumptions and boundaries. People, events, suppliers and quantities are illustrative and do not refer to real businesses.

## Interpretation and evidence

The assistant is not initially authorized to adjudicate claims or send customer decisions. Test examples are synthetic. An AI policy, framework crosswalk or aggregate metric does not establish conformity, fairness or safe deployment. Specialist model validation is not claimed by the author. The references support the framework context; proposed test methods and templates are illustrative analysis, not regulator-prescribed forms.

A source or worked example does not, by itself, demonstrate compliance. Distinguish a control’s design, observed operation, tested coverage and the authority to accept remaining risk. Source-review dates and licensed-text access limitations are recorded in the [source register](/references/source_register.md).
